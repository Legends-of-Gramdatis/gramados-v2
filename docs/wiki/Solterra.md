# Solterra

> **Status:** Draft for lore review. This article documents established in-world places and history, not the current availability of gameplay jobs. It is proposed wiki copy rather than a change to NPC dialogues or mechanics.

## Overview

**Solterra** is an island of Gramados distinguished by its old inland settlements, coastal development, pale-stone architecture, terracotta, agriculture, vineyards, and long-established local traditions. Its history is not centered on a single town: **Caldero** was historically the island's principal settlement and preserves some of its most ancient structures, while **Brisamar** became Solterra's largest town and foremost economic center following its rapid growth in the nineteenth century.

Both places retain important built heritage. Brisamar's modern prominence should not be mistaken for a recent foundation: the town contains old structures of considerable historical value alongside the commercial, cultural, and tourist facilities associated with its later expansion.

Solterra's chapels, craft traditions, fountains, roadside buildings, and landmarks are closely tied to local folklore. Stories involving the **Holy Seagull** and the **Sixth Wind** are prominent here, but neither is the whole of Solterran culture.

## Historical development

### Caldero and the older settlements

For much of Solterra's earlier history, **Caldero** was its main town. Some of the island's oldest surviving structures are found there, and its historic streets, public buildings, and market remain significant elements of Solterra's architectural heritage. The **Palacio de la Guardia**, **Paseo Caldero**, and **La Alhóndiga de Caldero** are among its recorded landmarks.

Caldero's **Fuente de los Ecos** is also a focal point of local life. Its importance is reflected in the story *A Fountain Once Lost*, in which a resident, Ernesto Ramiros, asks for help restoring the fountain's water supply. The story illustrates the continuing attachment of townspeople to an ordinary but cherished public place.

### Brisamar's nineteenth-century expansion

**Brisamar** was already an established settlement with its own historic structures before the nineteenth century. During that century, rapid economic growth and expanding tourism significantly increased its importance. Brisamar eventually overtook Caldero as the island's main economic center.

Today, Brisamar is Solterra's principal and largest town. Its tourism office, market, restaurants, school, cultural center, casino, and other amenities reflect its broad commercial and civic role. The nineteenth-century transformation did not erase its earlier heritage, and its older buildings remain part of the town's identity.

The precise commercial and transport developments that drove this expansion have yet to be documented in detail; they should not be presented as established facts without additional lore.

## Settlements and areas

### Brisamar

**Brisamar** is the island's principal town, economic center, and a major destination for visitors. It combines historic sites with an extensive range of modern civic, cultural, tourist, and commercial services. Its scale and importance distinguish it from the island's smaller settlements.

### Caldero

**Caldero** is Solterra's former principal town and an important center of historical architecture. Its old market, palace, fountain, and streets preserve the memory of the island's earlier development. Its heritage is complemented by shops and the everyday activity of a living town.

### Cántaro

**Cántaro** is strongly associated with pottery, clay, and kiln work. Local heritage includes the **Ermita del Barro Vivo**, **Capilla del Esmalte Fiel**, and **Los Tres Hornos del Voto**. The three ceremonial kilns bear the names **El Silente**, **El Paciente**, and **El Ardiente**. Their associated accounts describe the traditions of local potters and the importance of craftwork to the village.

### Lobrega

**Lobrega** is a rural village whose recorded landmarks include the **Ermita del Retiro Callado**. Its mix of occupied and abandoned buildings is part of its present character, though the causes of any decline should not be asserted without established historical sources.

### Calzadelad

**Calzadelad** is an established village on Solterra. Although it has not been represented in the earlier settlement summaries, it should be counted among the island's settlements. Further history and notable buildings remain to be documented.

### Golmera

**Golmera** is a hamlet on Solterra. Among its properties is **Taller de Ruta Sur**, a garage available for purchase. Hamlets such as Golmera are not necessarily labeled as settlements on Dynmap; the absence of a settlement marker does not imply that a place does not exist.

### Cresta Norte de Solterra

**Cresta Norte de Solterra** is part of the island's northern area, rather than a separate island. Its landmarks include the **Faro del Sexto Viento**, a lighthouse, and other structures such as the **Ermita de la Pluma Quieta**. The northern coast forms part of Solterra's geographic and architectural identity.

## Economy and industries

Solterra's economy includes **tourism, hospitality, farming, vineyards, pottery, shops, and vehicle-related services**. Economic activities differ between its major towns, villages, and smaller roadside communities.

### Tourism and commerce

Brisamar's economic rise is closely linked to the growth of tourism during the nineteenth century. Its present-day concentration of public facilities, businesses, and attractions reflects that enduring importance. Elsewhere, historic sites, small businesses, and roadside facilities support local activity.

### Viticulture

Winegrowing is an established feature of Solterra. Named vineyard estates include **Viñedos de Albaira** and **Dominio de Pedralba**. These are existing places in the world, even though their associated winemaking jobs have not yet been set up for player use. Solterra's viticulture should therefore be described as an in-world industry, not as a confirmed currently playable profession.

### Pottery and agriculture

Pottery is particularly associated with Cántaro and its kilns, chapels, and craft traditions. Farming and other rural trades also fit the island's established character.

### Trades and gameplay availability

Some Solterra professions are represented in backend job configurations or older test systems. Their presence in configuration files indicates historical implementation or intended economic themes, **not necessarily that players can currently access those jobs**. A lore article should describe relevant trades without promising specific mechanics or availability.

## Culture, traditions, and folklore

### El Sendero Blanco

**El Sendero Blanco** (*the White Path*) is an old pilgrimage route winding among Solterra's chapels, fountains, and elevated landmarks. Its surviving route markers encourage travelers to notice the landscape, water, and wind. Accounts of the route refer to the **Holy Seagull**, a recurring figure in Solterran local legends.

Pilgrimage is one element of the island's heritage, alongside practical traditions linked to craft, travel, food, and trade; it should not be used to characterize every aspect of Solterran life.

### The Six Winds and the Sixth Wind

The legend of the **Six Winds**, particularly the **Sixth Wind**, extends beyond Solterra and belongs to a wider body of Gramados folklore. Nevertheless, it has a particularly important place in Solterran history and local storytelling.

In Caldero, the **Bell of the Sixth Wind** is said to hang in the northern tower of the Palacio de la Guardia. The heritage account describes a bell whose supposed call is experienced through memory rather than ordinary sound, though it has not rung in living memory. Farther north, the **Faro del Sexto Viento** carries the same name. These landmarks demonstrate the legend's special prominence on the island without claiming that it originated there.

### Everyday heritage

Other surviving stories are rooted in familiar work and places. The **Capilla del Arranque** near Caldero, for example, is associated with the unexpected overnight repair of an old Trin Footpather and was subsequently adapted for use as a garage. Cántaro's stories tell of pottery surviving fire, water appearing during drought, and kilns preserved as ceremonial landmarks. These accounts are local legends, not independently verified historical events.
